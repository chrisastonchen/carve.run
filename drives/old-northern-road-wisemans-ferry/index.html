The drive

The Old Northern Road runs 43 km (27 mi) north from Dural to Wisemans Ferry, leaving Sydney's outer suburbs for the Hawkesbury sandstone plateau. It is a two-lane road with bends nearly all the way through bushland and orchards, and it drops at the end to the Hawkesbury River.

The road follows part of the Great North Road, which convicts built between 1825 and 1836 to link Sydney with the Hunter Valley. The convict road was an engineering feat but too steep and too short of water and feed to last, and parts of it fell out of use. The stretch you drive here is the working road that remained.

The drive stops before the river. At Wisemans Ferry a free vehicle ferry crosses the Hawkesbury, and the Devines Hill section of the Great North Road is on the far side for walkers.

Good to know

  • The ferry at Wisemans Ferry is a free state service running 24 hours, with a crossing of about 4 minutes; the drive ends before it.
  • The road is two lanes and undivided, with bends nearly all the way.
  • The road is a rural run of continuous bends, so allow the 40 minutes the drive takes and expect slower traffic.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check Live Traffic NSW.

Questions about this drive

How long is the Old Northern Road from Dural to Wisemans Ferry?

Dural to Wisemans Ferry on the Old Northern Road is 43 km (27 mi) and takes about 40 minutes.

Does the Old Northern Road have a toll or ferry fee?

No. The Old Northern Road has no toll, and the Wisemans Ferry vehicle ferry at its end is a free state service that runs 24 hours.

Who built the Old Northern Road?

Convicts built the Great North Road, of which the Old Northern Road is part, between 1825 and 1836 to connect Sydney with the Hunter Valley.
