The drive

Highway 79 is the gentle way up to Julian from I-8. From Descanso Junction it runs north into Cuyamaca Rancho State Park, through oak woodland and open meadows in the Cuyamaca Mountains, then along the north shore of Lake Cuyamaca, a reservoir run by the Helix Water District with boating and fishing.

North of the lake the highway carries on through the hills to Julian, an old mining town born in the gold rush of the 1870s and known now for its historic main street and its apple pie. The drive ends just short of Main Street.

It's driven north from Descanso, the usual approach from San Diego. Sunrise Highway, S1, joins Highway 79 near Lake Cuyamaca, so the two can be linked into a longer mountain day.

Good to know

  • Cuyamaca Rancho State Park charges a day-use fee per vehicle at its day-use areas, such as Stonewall Mine.
  • The park is open from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m.; Lake Cuyamaca, about two miles north of the park's Paso Picacho area, is run separately by the Helix Water District.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check Caltrans: Highway 79 conditions.

Questions about this drive

How long does Highway 79 from Descanso to Julian take?

Highway 79 from Descanso Junction to Julian is 22 miles and takes about 35 minutes without stops.

Is Highway 79 through Cuyamaca twisty?

Highway 79 through Cuyamaca Rancho State Park is mostly moderate curves, with three twistier stretches between Descanso and Julian.

Is Highway 79 through Cuyamaca open year-round?

Yes. Highway 79 is a state highway open year-round through Cuyamaca Rancho State Park, 15 miles south of Julian; Caltrans posts any roadwork or closures.

What is Julian known for?

Julian, at the north end of this Highway 79 drive, is an 1870s gold-rush town known for its historic buildings and its apple pie.
