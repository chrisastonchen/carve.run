The drive

Sol Duc Road leaves US 101 west of Lake Crescent and runs up the Sol Duc River valley inside Olympic National Park. Old-growth forest closes in on both sides, and the road is mostly sweeping, with a few tighter bends.

Salmon Cascades is about 5 miles up, where coho salmon jump the falls in fall and steelhead run in spring; late October and early November is a popular time to watch. The drive ends at Sol Duc Hot Springs, where the resort offers mineral pools, lodging and a restaurant. The trail to Sol Duc Falls starts at the end of the road, a little farther on, and is under a mile through old growth.

The drive is 11 miles and takes about 20 minutes. It pairs naturally with the Lake Crescent drive on US 101.

Good to know

  • A park entrance fee applies on this road.
  • The hot springs resort is generally open from late March through the last weekend in October, so its services close for the winter.
  • The road is a dead end, so you come back the way you went in.
  • Check conditions before you go; the park lists the Sol Duc area and road on its alerts page.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check Olympic National Park: alerts and conditions.

When is the Sol Duc Hot Springs resort open?

The resort is generally open from late March through the last weekend in October. Reservations are advised.
