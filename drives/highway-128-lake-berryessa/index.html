The drive

Highway 128 is the back way out of the Napa Valley to the east. This drive starts at Rutherford, in the middle of the valley's vineyards, crosses the valley floor and climbs Sage Canyon past Lake Hennessey, the City of Napa's reservoir, then on over dry ridges toward Lake Berryessa.

It skirts the south end of the lake, one of the largest reservoirs in California, which filled in the early 1960s and drowned the farming town of Monticello and the Berryessa Valley beneath it. At Monticello Dam, a 304-foot concrete arch finished in 1957, look for the Glory Hole, the lake's round spillway, which only runs when the lake is full; it last spilled in February 2025.

Below the dam the highway winds down the steep canyon of Putah Creek and comes out among the orchards at Winters, on the edge of the Sacramento Valley. It's a quiet road with sweeping curves for most of its length and a few tighter stretches.

Good to know

  • The Glory Hole spills only after very wet winters, when the lake rises above 440 feet; most of the time it's a concrete ring standing above the water.
  • Swimming near the Glory Hole is prohibited; view it from beside the highway at the dam.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check Caltrans highway conditions: Highway 128.

Questions about this drive

How long is the drive on Highway 128 from Rutherford to Winters?

Highway 128 from Rutherford in the Napa Valley to Winters is 38.3 miles and takes about an hour without stops.

Where can you see the Glory Hole at Lake Berryessa?

The Glory Hole spillway is at Monticello Dam, beside Highway 128 at the south end of Lake Berryessa; it only spills when the lake is full.

Is Highway 128 to Lake Berryessa twisty?

Highway 128 from Rutherford to Winters is mostly moderate, sweeping curves, with a few tighter stretches along the way.

When was Monticello Dam built?

Monticello Dam, which holds back Lake Berryessa beside Highway 128, was built between 1953 and 1957.
