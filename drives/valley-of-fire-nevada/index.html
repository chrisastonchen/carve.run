The drive

Valley of Fire State Park covers about 40,000 acres of bright red Aztec sandstone outcrops set in gray and tan limestone. The red sandstone dates to the Jurassic, the remnant of sand left by wind after inland seas receded. The park also holds petrified trees and petroglyphs more than 2,000 years old.

This drive enters from the east, where Northshore Road meets the Valley of Fire Highway, and runs 9.5 miles in about 30 minutes. It passes Elephant Rock and the visitor center, then follows White Domes Road to Rainbow Vista and ends near White Domes. The curves are mostly gentle. The visitor center has exhibits on the park's geology, ecology, prehistory and history, and the park recommends making it an early stop.

Good to know

  • Day use is $10 per vehicle, or $15 for non-Nevada vehicles.
  • The park closes to all visitors every year from December 1 through December 14.
  • Drive only on approved routes and park only in designated roadside spots.
  • Many trails, including White Domes Loop, close each year from May 15 to September 30 because of heat.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check Nevada State Parks: Valley of Fire State Park.

Questions about this drive

What does it cost to drive into Valley of Fire State Park?

Day use is $10 per vehicle, or $15 for non-Nevada vehicles.

When is Valley of Fire State Park closed?

The park closes to all visitors every year from December 1 through December 14 for maintenance.

Which entrance should I use at Valley of Fire?

The west entrance has closed on some days for construction. The east entrance, reached by State Route 169 or 167, is the alternative, so check the park's page before you go.

How long is the Valley of Fire drive?

This Valley of Fire drive is 9.5 miles and takes about 30 minutes without stops.
