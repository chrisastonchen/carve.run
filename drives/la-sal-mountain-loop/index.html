The drive

The La Sal Mountain Loop climbs from Spanish Valley, off Old Airport Road south of Moab, up through pinyon, oak and aspen onto the flanks of the La Sal Mountains. The high section tops out near 8,300 feet. Then tight switchbacks bring the road down into Castle Valley, and the drive finishes at Highway 128 beside the Colorado River. It is 35.7 miles and about 1 hr 10 min, with two twisty stretches.

The Forest Service describes the whole loop as a paved scenic backway. It is a quiet road with views of mesas and sandstone pinnacles such as Castle Rock.

From Highway 128 you can follow the river back to Moab.

Good to know

  • The Forest Service says the road is not suitable for large RVs or trailers because of tight switchbacks, especially on the north side toward Castle Valley.
  • There are no services on the route, so bring fuel, food and water.
  • Snow can pack the high section in winter, and the Forest Service page does not list a gate, so check conditions before going.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check Forest Service: La Sal Mountain Loop.

Questions about this drive

How long does the La Sal Mountain Loop take?

This drive from Spanish Valley to Highway 128 is 35.7 miles and takes about 1 hr 10 min.

Can you take an RV on the La Sal Mountain Loop?

The Forest Service says the road is not suitable for large RVs or trailers because of tight switchbacks.

Is the La Sal Mountain Loop paved?

Yes, the Forest Service describes it as a paved scenic backway.
