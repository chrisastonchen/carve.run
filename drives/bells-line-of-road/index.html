The drive

Bells Line of Road is the northern way over the Blue Mountains. This drive starts at North Richmond on Kurrajong Road and runs 71 km (44 mi) west to Lithgow, climbing out of the Hawkesbury Valley by Bellbird Hill, the steepest part of the road. The hill rises about 450 m to the top of the range on tight bends, and the climb carries on through Kurrajong Heights, where Bellbird Hill Lookout looks back toward the coast.

On the range the road settles into fruit country. Bilpin and Berambing are orchard districts, with apples the local crop, and Mount Tomah follows with the Blue Mountains Botanic Garden set high on the mountain. West of there the road runs through Blue Mountains National Park, with Mount Bell and Mount Charles beside it, before reaching Bell and turning onto Chifley Road for the descent to Lithgow.

The road was surveyed in 1823 and was little used until the 1939 to 1943 upgrade, made as a wartime alternative to the Great Western Highway. Today it is a quieter alternative to the highway, though trucks and weekend traffic still share it.

Good to know

  • The eastern climb up Bellbird Hill is steep, with tight bends; the grade is about 1 in 8.
  • The speed limit east of Bell is 80 km/h, and the ascents on both sides of Mount Tomah are steep.
  • Trucks and weekend traffic use the road, so overtaking chances are limited.
  • There is no toll. Roadworks with stop and slow traffic come and go along the road, so check before you leave.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check Live Traffic NSW.

Questions about this drive

How long does Bells Line of Road take to drive?

North Richmond to Lithgow on Bells Line of Road is 71 km (44 mi) and takes about 1 hr 15 min without stops.

Is Bells Line of Road a good alternative to the Great Western Highway?

Yes. Bells Line of Road was improved in 1939 to 1943 as a wartime alternative to the Great Western Highway, and it crosses the northern Blue Mountains by way of Kurrajong Heights, Bilpin and Mount Tomah.

Is Bells Line of Road twisty?

The steepest and tightest part is the climb up Bellbird Hill from the Hawkesbury Valley, and the road is steep on both sides of Mount Tomah. Much of the rest is gentler ridge-top driving.

What is there to see on Bells Line of Road?

Bellbird Hill Lookout above Kurrajong Heights, the orchards around Bilpin and the Blue Mountains Botanic Garden at Mount Tomah are the main stops on Bells Line of Road.
