The drive

The Coulee Corridor follows SR 17 and SR 155 through the Grand Coulee, a channel cut by Ice Age floods. This drive starts in Soap Lake and runs north, past the Lake Lenore Caves, where a short hike leads to basalt caves and overhangs, and the Dry Falls, a former waterfall about 400 feet high and 3.5 miles wide.

At Coulee City the route uses a short stretch of US 2 and then turns onto SR 155 along Banks Lake, a reservoir about 27 miles long. Steamboat Rock, a 650-foot basalt butte on the lake, is at Steamboat Rock State Park. The drive ends in the town of Grand Coulee.

The road itself is straight and easy, with almost no climbing. The basalt walls and the history of the floods are why people take it, and the byway has been a National Scenic Byway since 2005.

Good to know

  • Steamboat Rock State Park and Lake Lenore Caves are state lands; Washington State Parks says a Discover Pass is generally needed to park at state park day-use areas, but the Dry Falls Visitor Center lot does not require one.
  • Services are in Soap Lake, Coulee City and Grand Coulee; the stretch between has few.
  • Past closures on SR 155 have followed storms and slides, so check WSDOT before you go.
  • Banks Lake and the state parks are busiest on summer weekends.

Open all year, weather permitting. Roads close for snow, fire, storms and repairs: before you set off, check WSDOT travel map and alerts.

Questions about this drive

How long does the Coulee Corridor drive take?

Soap Lake to Grand Coulee on SR 17 and SR 155 is 49.8 miles and takes about 55 min.

What is there to see on the Coulee Corridor?

The route passes the Lake Lenore Caves, Dry Falls, Banks Lake and Steamboat Rock, all features of the Grand Coulee carved by Ice Age floods.

Is the Coulee Corridor twisty?

No. SR 17 and SR 155 are mostly straight, with gentle curves, and the climb over the whole drive is small.

Do you need a Discover Pass on the Coulee Corridor?

You need one to park at most Washington state parks along the way, such as Steamboat Rock State Park, though Washington State Parks says the Dry Falls Visitor Center does not require it. Driving the highway itself needs no pass.
