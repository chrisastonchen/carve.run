The drive

Highway 38 is the long way up to Big Bear, and the quiet one. It leaves Mentone as Mill Creek Road, runs up Mill Creek Canyon into the San Bernardino National Forest, and then climbs the canyon wall in a long run of curves to Angelus Oaks, on the ridge beneath the range's highest peaks.

Past Angelus Oaks the road levels out through Barton Flats, a stretch of pine forest and campgrounds, and follows the upper Santa Ana River past the turnoffs for Jenks Lake and the San Gorgonio trailheads. Then it climbs again to Onyx Summit at 8,443 feet, the highest mountain pass in Southern California, before dropping into Big Bear City.

The whole route is part of the Rim of the World Scenic Byway, which continues west on Highway 18. It's gentler than the Highway 18 approach, with one properly twisty section on the climb to Angelus Oaks, and it carries far less traffic.

Good to know

  • Storms have washed out sections of Highway 38 near Angelus Oaks, and repair work and winter weather can close it, so check Caltrans before you go.
  • Chains or snow tires may be required after winter storms, and Caltrans can turn back vehicles without them.

Questions about this drive

How long does Highway 38 from Mentone to Big Bear take?

Highway 38 from Mentone to Big Bear City is 43.2 miles and takes about an hour, climbing about 7,500 feet to Onyx Summit.

How high is Onyx Summit on Highway 38?

Onyx Summit on Highway 38 is 8,443 feet, the highest mountain pass in Southern California.

Is Highway 38 open to Big Bear?

Storm damage near Angelus Oaks has closed Highway 38 before, repair work can bring weekday closures, and winter storms can bring chain controls; check Caltrans highway conditions before you go.

Is Highway 38 or Highway 18 better to Big Bear?

Highway 38 from Mentone is longer but quieter and gentler than Highway 18, with fewer crowds and one twisty stretch on the climb to Angelus Oaks.
