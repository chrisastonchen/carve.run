Privacy Policy

Effective 28 October 2026

The short version. Carve uses your location on your phone to draw the map and guide your drives; the coordinates needed for maps, directions and search go to the mapping services that provide them. Weather comes from Apple Weather, which is told only areas about 11 km across — never your exact position. With an account, your routes are kept on Carve's servers so they're on all your devices, and they're private to you unless you share a route's link. If you turn on Group for a drive, the people sharing the same route near you see where you are, by name, until the drive ends; Carve keeps only your latest position, and deletes it when you stop. Reports you make while driving — police, a road hazard, people or animals on the road — are shown to other drivers where you made them; after a day they no longer say who made them. Carve has no ads and no advertising trackers. It keeps a record of which features and routes you use — never your coordinates — for 90 days, so we can see the app working, and you can turn that off. We don't sell data, and we never see your payment details.

Who we are

Carve is an app for planning and driving routes, for iPhone and CarPlay and for Android and Android Auto, made by Chris Aston Chen (“Carve”, “we”). This policy explains what the apps do with information, and what your choices are. Questions: support@carve.run.

Your account

Carve needs an account, which also keeps your routes in sync across your devices. You sign in with Apple, with Google, or with your email address and a six-digit code Carve emails you. From that sign-in Carve's backend keeps an account identifier and what your provider passes on: with Google, your email address, name and Google profile picture; with Apple, nothing more — Carve doesn't ask Apple for your name or email; with email, the address you gave.

Your account also has a username you choose, and optionally a display name and a profile picture. These are how you appear to other people: other signed-in Carve users can see them, they appear on routes you share, and your profile picture is stored at a public web address. Profile pictures are re-encoded when you choose them, which removes location and camera details from the photo.

You can also add a photo to a route you made, taken with your camera or chosen from your library. Carve sees only the photo you pick. It's re-encoded at the size you frame it, which removes location and camera details, then checked automatically before anyone else can see it; a photo that fails the check is deleted. Once added, it's stored at a public web address and shown as the route's picture to anyone you share the route with. You can change or remove it at any time. If you report someone's photo, Carve keeps the report — which photo, and your account — to review it, and hides that photo on your device.

If a route you made has no photo of its own, Carve looks for one: a freely licensed photo from Wikimedia Commons taken near the route. To find it, the app sends Carve's server up to four points from the middle of the route — never where it starts or ends — rounded to about a kilometre, with the towns at its ends, the names of its roads and the route's name. Carve's server, not your device, asks Wikimedia Commons for photos taken near those points, and for photos named after the route's roads and the places in its name, and doesn't keep the points. A photo found this way is copied to a public web address and shown with its photographer's name and licence, which links to the photo's page. You can remove it, or replace it with your own; once removed, Carve doesn't look again for that route.

Your routes

  • On your device. Routes you plan, save, record or import — their names, start and end points, stops, the road path and turn instructions computed for them, their elevation profile and description, and when you last drove them — are stored in the app.
  • In your account. When you're signed in, each route is kept on Carve's servers as well, so it's on every device you sign in to. Routes in your account are private to you. When you delete a route, it's removed from your devices and marked deleted on our servers; that server copy is erased when you delete your account.
  • Drives in progress. Which stops you've reached and your last position on a route are kept only on the device, so an interrupted drive can pick up where it left off. Drive history isn't sent to our servers, except what goes with a review you send (below).
  • Importing. A GPX file you import is read on your device. For a Ride with GPS link, Carve's server asks Ride with GPS for that route with Carve's own access and passes back its name, its path and its points of interest; Ride with GPS isn't told who asked. Only routes anyone with the link may see come through: public ones, and unlisted ones whose link carries their code.
  • Recording a drive. While you record, the trace of where you drive stays on the device. When you end a recording with a connection, the trace (thinned to a few hundred points) is sent to Mapbox to match it to the roads, and the result becomes a route like any other.
  • Settings and search history stay on the device.

Sharing routes

Every route you keep in your account has a link — carve.run followed by a short code — that opens it. Anyone who has the link can see the route — its name, points, stops, road path, description and photo — and your username and profile picture as the person who made it. Links you send are outside Carve's control. Links never include your location or when you've driven the route.

When a link is shared in a message, carve.run shows a preview — the route's name, length and a small map of it, drawn by Mapbox.

Rating your drives

After a drive, Carve may ask how it was, with a notification or when you next open the app. Answering is optional. If you send a review, your account keeps your rating, the reasons you picked, any note you write, your verdict on each road you rate, when you drove, how much of the route you covered and whether you finished it. Reviews are private to you: nobody else sees them. In time, Carve may combine many drivers' reviews into a route's overall rating, without your name or note. Your reviews are erased when you delete your account.

Group: driving together

Group, part of Carve Apex, lets people driving the same route see each other on the map. It's off unless you turn it on, for one route at a time, from the route's preview or during the drive.

  • What others see. While Group is on and you're within about 3 miles of the route, your phone sends Carve's server your position every few seconds (every 20 seconds while you're stopped), with your heading, speed, how far along the route you are, whether you're stopped and which way you're driving it. Other Carve users sharing the same route — or a copy of it — see your position, wherever they are along it, on their map and car screen, with your display name (or username) and profile picture, how far ahead or behind you are, and how long ago you last updated.
  • Only while you share, and only nearby. You see others only while you're sharing yourself. Further than about 3 miles from the route your phone stops sending and your position is removed, so turning Group on at home doesn't reveal it unless you live near the route. The server checks the distance itself rather than leaving it to the app, but it can only check the position a phone reports — it can't tell whether that's true.
  • No history. The server keeps one record for you: your latest position on the route. It's deleted when you turn Group off, end the drive or leave the route, and if your phone loses its connection first, others see it as where you were last seen for up to 30 minutes, after which it's deleted. Carve doesn't keep a record of where you've driven.
  • Are you driving in a group? When you open a route near people already sharing it, the app may ask whether you'd like to join them. To ask, it sends the server the route and where you are, and gets back only how many people are sharing within a mile — never who or where. Your position isn't stored; the server keeps only when your account last asked, so it can be asked at most every 15 seconds.
  • Hide and Report. You can hide any driver: neither of you will see the other again. Hidden drivers are listed under Me, where you can show them again. You can report a driver; Carve keeps the report — who, on which route, and your account — to review it.

Your name in a group is the display name you set under Me, or your username. Deleting your account deletes your position, hidden drivers and reports with it.

Reporting what's on the road

During a drive you can report what's on the road ahead — police, a crash, something in the road, a bump or pothole, or people or animals — from the drive screen, the car's screen or the lock screen. Nothing is reported unless you tap to report it.

  • What a report holds. What you reported, where you were when you tapped, which way you were heading, when, the route you were driving, and your account. A report made without signal waits on your phone and is sent when you're back in range; one that's too old to matter by then isn't sent.
  • Who sees it. Police, crashes, things in the road and bumps or potholes are shown as a pin on the map to anyone using Carve who looks at that area, and told of along the road to drivers coming up to them. People and animals are shown only to drivers on the same route as you who are driving it in Group. Those drivers see your name with a report while it's less than a day old; everyone else sees it without a name. When several drivers report the same thing, others see how many reported it.
  • Still there? A driver who passes a report may be asked whether it's still there. Carve keeps each answer with that driver's account for a day. After that the answer is kept, until the report is deleted, only with a one-way code made from the account and the report, which can't be turned back into the account, so nobody is counted twice.
  • How long it's kept. A report stands until it's said to be gone or its time runs out: 10 minutes for people and animals; up to two hours for police, crashes and things in the road; a year for bumps and potholes, or longer while drivers keep saying they're still there. After 24 hours a report no longer records whose it was: your account, and the route you were on, are removed from it, leaving what was reported, where and when. Reports are deleted a week after they're taken down or run out.
  • On your phone. The reports on a route are kept on your phone, so you're told of them without signal, for routes you've driven recently and routes you've downloaded, until each one runs out.

You can undo a report right after making it. Deleting your account deletes the reports and answers it made in the last day; older ones already no longer record your account.

How you use the app

So we can see whether Carve is working for the people using it, the app records usage events and sends them to Carve's server: when it's opened and how long it stays open; signing in and out, and whether the account is new; routes opened, created, imported, edited, renamed, deleted, shared and downloaded, and which route each is about; drives started, finished or ended early, with how long they took, how much of the route they covered and whether they used CarPlay or Android Auto; leaving and rejoining a route; recordings; Group turned on and off; and Carve Apex offers, purchases and restores, and whether a subscription is in its free trial, paid or ended.

Each event carries your account (when you're signed in), a random identifier made for the app on your phone, the app's version, and whether it's an iPhone or Android phone. It never includes your coordinates, what you search for or a route's name. But events about a route say which route, so events about drives show which route you drove and when. Events wait on your phone until there's a connection.

They're used only to understand and improve Carve — never for advertising, and never sold or shared. They're kept for 90 days, then deleted, and deleting your account deletes yours. To stop them, turn off Share Usage with Carve under Me; anything not yet sent is discarded.

The first time you open Carve, it also asks Apple, through Apple's own AdServices, whether you installed it after tapping one of Carve's Apple Ads. If you did, one usage event records the numbers Apple gives for the campaign, ad group and keyword, and your country or region; if not, it records that no ad was involved. Apple doesn't tell Carve who you are, and Carve doesn't use your phone's advertising identifier or ask to track you. It's used only to see which of Carve's own ads work, and it's skipped when Share Usage with Carve is off. Unlike other usage events, this one is kept with your account until you delete it, together with when you started a free trial of Carve Apex, first paid for it and whether it has ended, so we can see over a year which ads bring people who stay.

This website

carve.run counts visits with Plausible Analytics, so we can see where Carve's visitors come from: each page viewed, how long it stayed open and how far it was scrolled, and each tap on an App Store or Android beta button, with the page, the website you came from, any campaign tags on the link you followed (such as utm_source), your browser, device type and country. Plausible doesn't use cookies or keep your IP address: it tells one visitor from another on the same day with a code made from your IP address and browser that changes every day and can't be traced back to you. A shared route's link never goes to Plausible; its page is counted as a share page, without the route. Visits aren't counted from browsers that send Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

The App Store links on this site carry a campaign tag, so Apple's App Store Connect can tell us which link led to an install. Apple reports that to us in totals, not about you.

Location

Carve asks for location access to centre the map, to plan from where you are, and to guide a drive or record one. During a drive or a recording it tracks your position continuously — on Android with a notification showing while it does, on iPhone in the background while the drive is active — so it can announce turns, clear stops, and tell when you've left the route. Carve doesn't ask for location access at other times in the background.

Your location is processed on the device and is not sent to Carve's servers, except while you use Group, as described above, and — on Android, rounded to an area about 11 km across — to get the weather where you are during a drive (see Weather, below). Coordinates go to the services below only as needed to do what you asked. You can withdraw location access at any time in your phone's settings; the map still works, but drives can't be guided.

Describing a drive

You can describe a drive, or a change to one, in your own words — “a twisty hour, coffee halfway, back by 5” — typed or spoken. Speech is turned into text on your phone; the recording never leaves it. What you asked goes to Carve's server with the route as words — its name, length and time, the names of its roads, stops and towns, and the names of places the app found for it, with how far and how long — and the server asks Anthropic what to change, and then how to tell you what changed. Anthropic never receives coordinates, and nothing that identifies you: finding places and working out routes happen on your phone, with the services below. While Share Usage with Carve is on (under Me), Carve's server keeps what you asked for 90 days, with how it was read, what the app did, what Carve replied and your thumbs up or down if you give one, so we can see where describing a drive goes wrong and fix it. It's kept with your account if you're signed in, never with coordinates or your IP address, and it's deleted with your account. Turn it off and nothing you ask is kept. To keep the free allowance fair, the server counts requests by account, or by IP address if you're not signed in.

Weather

Carve shows forecasts from Apple Weather: along a route, for when you'll reach each part of it; on the map, when you turn on its Weather layer; and during a drive, for where you are and what's ahead. To get them, the app asks for the forecast at a few places — points along the route, the parts of the map in view, and during a drive where you are — but each is first rounded to the middle of an area about 11 km (7 miles) across, so Apple Weather is never told your exact position or your route's exact path. On iPhone the app asks Apple Weather itself; on Android it asks Carve's server, which asks Apple Weather and keeps nothing; with those areas it sends your phone's time zone (so days start at your midnight) and the country those areas are in (so official weather warnings for them can be shown), and nothing else about you. Forecasts are kept on your device for a day, so a drive has its weather without signal.

Where your data goes

Carve is run from the United States. Carve's servers are in the United States (Supabase, in Oregon), and most of the services above process what they receive there too; Plausible keeps the website's counts in the EU. So if you live outside the United States — in Australia or New Zealand, for example — what Carve keeps and what these services receive is sent to and stored in other countries, whose privacy laws may not protect it in the same way as yours. Carve sends each service only what the table above says, only for the reason it gives, and handles your information as this policy describes wherever it's stored.

Carve Apex and purchases

Subscriptions are sold through the App Store on iPhone and Google Play on Android, which handle payment and billing; Carve never sees your card or bank details. Each purchase is tied to your Carve account — on iPhone with your account identifier, on Android with a one-way hash of it — so that it follows your account. For the Carve Apex features that run on Carve's server, such as Popular times, the app sends the store's signed record of your subscription, and Carve's server keeps which subscription your account has: the plan, the store's identifier for the purchase (on Android, a one-way hash of it), whether it's a test purchase, when it ends, and whether it was refunded. Apple tells Carve's server when a subscription renews, ends or is refunded. When you delete your account, that record is no longer linked to it.

What Carve does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising or cross-app tracking identifiers.
  • No third-party analytics or crash-reporting services in the app: usage events, described above, go only to Carve's own server. The website uses Plausible, as described above. If you've chosen to share analytics with developers in your phone's settings, Apple or Google may share aggregated crash reports with us.
  • No selling, renting, or trading of personal information.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it. You can delete a route in the app, remove offline maps from a route's menu, and sign out at any time. Delete Account, in the app under Me, permanently erases your account, profile, profile picture, route photos, routes (including ones you'd deleted) and their links, your route reviews, and your Group position, hidden drivers, reports, the last day's road reports and answers, and usage events from Carve's servers, removes them from that device, and revokes Sign in with Apple. Route descriptions and voice recordings that aren't linked to anyone are kept.

On iPhone, the app's data is included in your device backups according to your iOS settings; your sign-in stays on the device. On Android, the app's data isn't backed up, except your sign-in in Google's Block Store, which signing out or deleting your account removes.

Children

Carve is a driving app and is not directed at children under 16. We don't knowingly collect information from them.

Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, export or erase your personal data, and to complain to a supervisory authority. Most of it you can change or delete in the app; for anything else, write to support@carve.run and we'll help.

If you think we've mishandled your information, please write to us first so we can put it right. You can also complain to your privacy regulator: in Australia, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner; in New Zealand, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner; in the EU or UK, your data protection authority.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the effective date above moves and the app will point you to the new version. Earlier versions are available on request.
