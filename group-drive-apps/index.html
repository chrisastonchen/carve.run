The best group drive apps for 2026

By Chris Aston Chen · Updated November 2026

A group drive needs three things from an app: everyone on the same route, everyone on the same map, and nobody lost when the signal drops. Some apps do the route, some do the map, and a few run the sign-ups around it. Here's what each one does for a club run, a convoy or a weekend rally.

The short answer. Carve, for the drive: plan the route once, share one link, and every driver, on iPhone or Android, gets turn-by-turn on the same route, with everyone on one map and the car's screen, free. Road & Rally if you also want RSVPs and paid registration in the same app; MotorsportReg for track-event sign-ups.

1. Carve Our app

Best for club runs and group drives

Plan the route once, in the app or on a computer at carve.run/plan, or import the club's GPX or Ride with GPS route, then share one link or QR code. Every driver, on iPhone, Android or in a browser, gets the same route with spoken turn-by-turn, and your later edits reach them. With Group, everyone on the route shows on the map and the car's screen, with how far ahead or behind they are. With Carve Club, organizers plan each day's route, meet place and time on the web, and every driver gets Apex free on the club's routes.

Price: Free for drivers. Club: $499 a year after a 30-day free trial, with Apex free for every driver on the club's routes. Platforms: iPhone with CarPlay, Android with Android Auto, web planner. See Carve

2. Road & Rally

Best for hosting meets and rallies

Road Finder grades roads on curvature and flow, Convoy puts the group on a shared live map, and organizers can host meets and multi-day rallies with RSVPs and paid registration. Turn-by-turn and Convoy are free; VIP adds offline maps and full curated guides. It lists CarPlay on iPhone, not Android Auto.

Price: Free; VIP is an in-app purchase. Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Android. Carve vs Road & Rally

3. Joinr

Best for organizing a car event in one app

A free app for car events and rally trips: everyone's location during the event, one-tap alerts and voice messages. Its listings don't describe turn-by-turn, offline maps, CarPlay or Android Auto.

Price: Free. Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Android. Carve vs Joinr

4. calimoto

Best for motorcycle riders

A motorcycle app with a free twisty-roads planner and a Calimeter that rates how curvy a ride is. Turn-by-turn, offline maps, group rides, CarPlay and Android Auto come with Premium.

Price: Free planning; Premium $79.99 a year after a 14-day trial, or $12.99 a week. Platforms: iPhone, Android, web planner. Carve vs calimoto

5. REVER

Best for a riders' community

A motorcycle-first app with thousands of routes, mostly from riders. Turn-by-turn, twisty routing, offline maps and live location sharing are Pro, and there's no Android Auto.

Price: Free; Pro $39.99 a year after a 14-day trial. Platforms: iPhone, Android, web. Carve vs REVER

6. Ride with GPS

Best for cycling clubs

A cycling route planner with over a million routes, and club and event accounts that give members turn-by-turn on the club's routes. Live Logging shares your own ride; CarPlay and Android Auto aren't listed. Its routes import straight into Carve for a drive.

Price: Starter is free; Premium after a 7-day free trial. Platforms: iPhone, Apple Watch, Android, web. Carve vs Ride with GPS

7. MotorsportReg

Best for finding and signing up for track events

The place to find and register for track days, HPDE, autocross and other motorsport events, and for organizers to run them. It's for the event, not the drive to it.

Price: Free to search events. Platforms: Web.

8. Google Maps

Best for sharing where you are

Location sharing shows people who share with you, and anyone can follow directions to a meet point. There's no group-drive mode, and it re-routes each driver separately on traffic, so a group soon ends up on different roads.

Price: Free. Platforms: iPhone, Android, web. Carve vs Google Maps

How we picked

  • One route for everyone. Can every driver open the same multi-stop route, on iPhone and Android, without re-sending it when it changes?
  • The group on one map. Can drivers see each other while they drive, and on the car's screen?
  • No signal, no problem. Does the route keep working in the canyon?
  • What drivers have to pay. A club drive falls apart if half the group hits a paywall.

Carve is our app; we've listed it first because it's built for this, and said what each of the others is best for. Questions

What's the best app for a car club drive?

Carve: the organizer plans one route and shares one link, every driver gets turn-by-turn on the same route on iPhone or Android, and Group shows everyone on one map, free. Carve Club adds planning by day on the web and Apex free for the club's drivers.

How do I keep a group of cars together on a drive?

Plan one route with regroup stops every 45 to 60 minutes, share it so everyone has the same turns, and use a lead and a sweep car. See how to plan and lead a group drive.

Is there a free convoy app?

Yes. Carve's Group (everyone on the route on one map, with how far ahead or behind they are) and Road & Rally's Convoy are both free; calimoto's and REVER's group features are on their paid plans.

Which group drive app handles sign-ups and payments?

Road & Rally takes RSVPs and paid registration, and MotorsportReg runs registration for track and motorsport events. Carve doesn't take sign-ups: share the Carve route link wherever your club already registers drivers.

Can drivers on Android and iPhone use the same route?

In Carve, yes: one link opens the route on iPhone, Android or in a browser, with CarPlay and Android Auto both supported.
