Carve vs Google Maps

For scenic drives, car clubs and group drives · Checked October 2026

Carve (carve.run) is a scenic-drive planner and turn-by-turn navigation app for cars and motorcycles, on iPhone with CarPlay and Android with Android Auto — the Google Maps alternative for scenic drives: it keeps the road you picked instead of rerouting you onto the fast one.

The short answer. Google Maps is the best everyday navigation app there is — and that's the problem on a scenic drive. It's built to get you there fastest, so it keeps re-routing you toward the quicker road. Carve is built for the drive itself: it finds the twisty, scenic roads, drives exactly the route you planned without ever swapping it for a faster one, puts your whole group on one map, and with Apex keeps every turn working where there's no signal. You can even bring your Google Maps route with you: paste its link into Carve.

Where Carve pulls ahead

  • It never reroutes you onto the fast road. Carve drives exactly the route you planned. Google Maps re-routes on live traffic, which on a scenic drive means toward the highway you were trying to avoid.
  • Wrong turn? Back to your road. Carve gives turn-by-turn directions back to where you'll rejoin your route, instead of working out a new one from wherever you are.
  • The good roads, found. 1,100+ hand-picked drives rated Leisurely to Intense, a planner that leans toward scenic roads, and great driving roads marked on the map. Google Maps can avoid highways, but it can't tell a canyon road from a frontage road.
  • The whole group on one map. Share one link and everyone drives the same route; with Group, everyone on it shows by name on the map and the car's screen, with how far ahead or behind they are.
  • More stops, kept in sync. Shape a route with up to 100 points between start and finish, and edits to a shared route reach everyone — no re-sending links.
  • Every turn with no signal. With Apex, Carve downloads the map and every turn of the route for the canyons and passes where signal drops.

When Google Maps is the better pick

For the commute, errands, finding a restaurant, transit or walking, Google Maps is hard to beat, and it's free. Carve isn't trying to replace it for getting somewhere fast — it's for the drives where the road is the point.

Questions

Is Carve better than Google Maps for scenic drives?

Yes. Google Maps is built to find the fastest way and re-routes on traffic. Carve finds the twisty, scenic roads, then guides you along exactly that route, turn by turn, and never swaps it for a faster one.

How do I stop Google Maps from rerouting me?

Google Maps doesn't have a setting that locks a route; adding stops shapes it, but it can still re-route on traffic. Carve keeps your route by design — paste your Google Maps route link into Carve's Import and drive it there.

Can I import my Google Maps route into Carve?

Yes, free. Share the route in Google Maps, copy the link, and paste it into Carve's Import; the stops come with it.

Which is better for a group drive or car club run?

Carve. Share one link and everyone opens the same route on iPhone or Android, and your later edits reach them. With Group, everyone driving it shows on the map and the car's screen by name. Google Maps has location sharing, but no group drive.

Does Carve work offline like Google Maps?

Yes, with Carve Apex: each route downloads with its map and every turn, so the planned route works the whole way with no signal.

How many stops can I add?

Google Maps allows up to 9 stops including the destination. A Carve route can have up to 100 points between start and finish, 10 of them named stops.

Is Carve free?

Planning, importing, sharing and driving with a signal are free. Carve Apex — $29.99 a year with a 7-day free trial, or $4.99 a month — adds offline maps, Group, CarPlay and Android Auto, 3D previews and more.
