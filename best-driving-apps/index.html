The best driving apps for 2026

By Chris Aston Chen · Updated November 2026

"Best driving app" depends on the drive. The app that gets you to work fastest is the wrong one on a canyon road on Sunday morning, and neither is any use at a track day. Here's what each of the apps drivers actually use is best at, with prices and platforms, so you can pick the right one, or the right two.

The short answer. For everyday driving, Google Maps (or Waze if traffic is your enemy). For driving for the fun of it, and especially with friends or a car club, Carve: it plans the drive on great roads, drives exactly that route without rerouting you, and keeps the whole group on one map, free. For a co-driver calling each corner, Rods; for motorcycles, calimoto; for track days, RaceChrono Pro.

1. Carve Our app

Best for scenic drives and group drives

Plan a drive on great roads, from 1,200+ hand-picked drives or stop by stop in the app or on a computer at carve.run/plan, or import a GPX file, a Ride with GPS link or a Google Maps link. Share one link and every driver gets spoken turn-by-turn on exactly that route; Carve never reroutes you onto a faster road, and after a wrong turn it leads you forward to where you rejoin it. With Group, everyone on the route is on one map, and on CarPlay or Android Auto.

Price: Free. Individual (Apex for one driver), $49.99 a year with a 14-day free trial, adds offline maps, 3D previews and road conditions. Platforms: iPhone with CarPlay, Android with Android Auto, web planner. See Carve

2. Google Maps

Best for everyday navigation

Live traffic, up to 9 stops, offline directions inside downloaded areas, CarPlay and Android Auto. It's built to get you there fastest and re-routes on traffic, so on a road you picked for the corners it keeps pulling you toward the highway.

Price: Free. Platforms: iPhone, Android, web. Carve vs Google Maps

3. Waze

Best for beating traffic

Crowd-sourced traffic, police and hazard reports, and quick rerouting around jams, on CarPlay and Android Auto. One stop per route, and no downloadable offline maps listed.

Price: Free, with ads. Platforms: iPhone, Android. Carve vs Waze

4. Apple Maps

Best built-in map on iPhone

Up to 14 driving stops, offline turn-by-turn inside downloaded areas (iOS 17+) and CarPlay. No scenic-road option, no Android app and no way to send a multi-stop route to a friend.

Price: Free, built into Apple devices. Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac. Carve vs Apple Maps

5. Rods

Best for corner-by-corner pace notes

A pocket co-driver: it calls each corner aloud before you reach it, rated Easy/Medium/Hard or on the rally 1-6 scale, plus speed bumps, surface changes and 80,000+ speed cameras, and on CarPlay it can show the next corner on supported instrument clusters. Planning is free; driving with its callouts is a 14-day trial, then a subscription. Its listings don't mention group drives or Android Auto.

Price: Free to plan; the US App Store lists yearly plans from $35.90, or $9.59 a month. Platforms: iPhone with CarPlay, Android. Carve vs Rods

6. calimoto

Best for motorcycle riders

A motorcycle app with a free twisty-roads planner and a Calimeter that rates how curvy a ride is. Turn-by-turn, offline maps, group rides, CarPlay and Android Auto come with Premium.

Price: Free planning; Premium $79.99 a year after a 14-day trial, or $12.99 a week. Platforms: iPhone, Android, web planner. Carve vs calimoto

7. Road & Rally

Best for hosting meets and rallies

Road Finder grades roads on curvature and flow, Convoy puts the group on a shared live map, and organizers can host meets and multi-day rallies with RSVPs and paid registration. Turn-by-turn and Convoy are free; VIP adds offline maps and full curated guides. It lists CarPlay on iPhone, not Android Auto.

Price: Free; VIP is an in-app purchase. Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Android. Carve vs Road & Rally

8. Roadtrippers

Best for road-trip stops

A road-trip planner for places, attractions and campgrounds along the way. Turn-by-turn and CarPlay come with paid plans, offline maps don't give directions, and it doesn't support Android Auto.

Price: Free for 1 trip with 3 stops; paid plans from $35.99 a year. Platforms: iPhone, Android, web. Carve vs Roadtrippers

9. Kurviger

Best curvy-route planner for riders

Curvy and extra-curvy route profiles and round trips, free to plan. Navigation, offline maps and recording are on its paid Tourer+ plan; CarPlay and Android Auto need a subscription.

Price: Free planning; Tourer €14.99 and Tourer+ €29.99 a year. Platforms: iPhone, Android, web. Carve vs Kurviger

10. RaceChrono Pro

Best for track days

A lap timer and data logger for motorsport, with lap and sector timing, 2,600+ tracks, support for external GPS receivers and OBD-II readers, and video with your data overlaid. It's for the circuit, not the public road.

Price: $19.99 on the US App Store. Platforms: iPhone, iPad.

How we picked

  • Does it keep the route you chose? Everyday apps re-route you toward the fastest road; on a drive you planned for the road itself, that's the opposite of what you want.
  • Does it work where the good roads are? Mountain passes and canyons lose signal, so offline maps and directions matter.
  • Does it work in the car? CarPlay and Android Auto put the route on the car's screen instead of a phone on the dash.
  • Can your friends use it too? If half the group is on Android, an iPhone-only app leaves them out.
  • What's free? Some apps are free to plan and charge to drive.

Carve is our app; we've listed it first because it's built for this, and said what each of the others is best for. What this page says about other apps comes from their own store listings and pages, checked in November 2026; each comparison page lists its sources. Questions

What's the best app for driving for fun?

Carve, if the road is the point: it has 1,200+ hand-picked driving roads, plans routes that lean toward scenic, twisty roads, and drives them turn by turn without ever swapping in a faster road, free. Everyday apps like Google Maps and Waze are built to find the fastest way and re-route you toward it.

Which driving app doesn't reroute you?

Carve keeps the route you planned by design: it never reroutes onto a faster road, and after a wrong turn it leads you forward to where you rejoin the route. Google Maps, Apple Maps and Waze have no setting that locks a route.

What's the best app for driving with a group of cars?

Carve. Share one link and everyone, on iPhone or Android, drives the same route, and with Group everyone on it shows on the map and the car's screen, free. See the best group drive apps.

Which driving apps work offline?

Carve with Apex downloads the map and every turn of a route. Google Maps and Apple Maps give directions inside downloaded areas, and Rods, calimoto (Premium), Kurviger (Tourer+) and Road & Rally (VIP) have offline modes. Waze lists no downloadable maps.

Which driving apps work with CarPlay and Android Auto?

Carve, Google Maps, Waze and calimoto (Premium) list both. Apple Maps, Rods and Road & Rally list CarPlay only; Roadtrippers has CarPlay on paid plans.

Are the best driving apps free?

Google Maps, Waze and Apple Maps are free. Carve is free to plan, share and drive with turn-by-turn; its Individual plan, $49.99 a year, adds offline maps. Rods, calimoto and Kurviger are free to plan but charge for driving with navigation.
